# BlackPearl

A Windows desktop preview for watching local movies together. C# / WPF, LibVLCSharp, SQLite and native libdatachannel. Only whoever plays a video for a room needs its file.

## Run the app

Open `artifacts/BlackPearl/BlackPearl.exe` after publishing, or build from source below. The self-contained publish includes the .NET runtime, libVLC, and app-local Microsoft Visual C++ x64 runtime dependencies; installing VLC or copying runtime DLLs separately is unnecessary.

1. On first launch, choose **Add my videos** to set up folders and scan your library, or **Join a friend** to enter an invitation. Only whoever plays a video needs its file.
2. Browse the poster grid, open a series, then a season, then an episode. On Windows, selecting a movie or episode opens its own information page with artwork, metadata, and **Play locally** / **Watch together** actions. **Back to library** restores the same season, or the same search and filters.
3. Artwork and metadata are fetched automatically after scanning. Configurable providers try TMDB (with the bundled BlackPearl application key or an optional personal override), TVmaze, and Wikipedia in priority order. NFO files and locked fields take priority. Settings offer fill missing, refresh text, replace downloaded artwork, and retry missing artwork, plus language, refresh interval, and optional missing/upcoming episodes. Outstanding matching and artwork issues remain visible during retry cooldowns. Shows and seasons have stable identities; filename aliases in one show folder stay together, while same-name remakes stay separate. See [metadata and library behavior](METADATA.md) for numbering, duplicate versions, NFO support, and migration details.
4. For a room, enter a deployed HTTPS server in Settings. Create the room and copy its code; anyone who has it pastes it under **Join a room** and joins straight away, with no approval, and the room stays open whether or not its creator is in it. The Rooms page shows who is in each saved room before you enter, and double-clicking a room's name joins it. **Join voice** on a room there enters it with voice chat on; in the room, the same button joins or leaves voice. **Watch together** on a title plays it for the room you are in, from anyone's library: starting a video takes over the stream, after asking when someone else's is playing. The room's creator can rename it and remove people, and let them back in. Settings, including the TMDB key, apply as you change them; typed values apply after a short pause, and a value that isn't valid shows its error and isn't applied until it is fixed.
5. Everyone in a room can play, pause, seek and change tracks by default; whoever is playing the video can turn that off with **Everyone can control** in the player, or per room on the Rooms page. Everyone follows the streamer's audio and subtitle selections; volume remains personal. Buffering pauses everyone and resumes when participants report ready.
6. In a room, Library, Home and Queues show your own, and playing one of your queues takes over the stream. When whoever holds the streaming role has **Browse my library** on (Watch rooms), the Library tab offers **Browse their library**: one person at a time looks through it, can start a title with **Play in room**, and sees that person's saved queues on the Queues page, where they can reorder and remove videos, create queues, and add videos with **Add to queue**. The owner's app applies each change to its own queues and shares the result; only the owner plays, renames and deletes them. **Back to my library** returns to your own.

In Windows **Settings → Playback**, choose audio and subtitle languages from the lists, then use the arrows to arrange their priority. Add subtitle rules for specific audio languages: for example, Japanese audio can prefer English, then Russian, then **Off**, while English audio uses **Off**. The default subtitle list applies to audio languages without a rule. Remove all languages from a list to keep the video's original defaults. Saved preferences apply to the next video, and manual subtitle choices last for that video. When a local file doesn't name a track's language (including Matroska tracks with no language tag, which players otherwise show as English), BlackPearl identifies it offline with bundled models: Whisper tiny listens to audio from the first minutes, CLD3 reads text subtitles in Matroska and MP4 files, and Tesseract reads picture subtitles (Blu-ray PGS and DVD VobSub in Matroska) for CLD3 to name. Such tracks show *identifying language* for a few seconds, then the language found and *detected*; preferences wait for the result, which is remembered for the file. To skip that wait, identification starts early: when you open a video's page, which lists its audio and subtitles with their languages, and for the next episode or queued video while one plays. Subtitle files take the language their name ends with, as in `Movie.ru.srt` or `Movie.en.forced.srt`, or else the language of their text; this covers files loaded from the player, files shared in a room, and files beside the video that play automatically (those are named only when it's clear which track each file became). Untagged tracks that can't be identified, such as a sign track with a few words, show as *Unknown language* rather than the English players assume.

Library pages, posters, queue changes and start requests travel directly over WebRTC. The streamer's queues reach the others in chunks that stay under the room's message limit; a change names the queue it was made against, so a change to a queue that has since changed is refused with a reason instead of landing on the wrong video. While someone browses your library, you see where they are and keep browsing it yourself; **Follow** shows what they see, and **Stop following** or scrolling returns to your own view. Browsing presence expires after connection loss, and you can end a browsing session at any time.

Room playback uses a dedicated, reliable WebRTC control channel alongside the media channel. A room has no host. Every member holds a direct connection to every other, and one member at a time holds the streaming role: its app keeps playback revisions, readiness and queue state, and the others synchronize to its clock and send it their commands for authorization and distribution. The role moves to whoever starts a video, with no reconnection, and passes to whoever has been in the room longest when its holder leaves; their video ends, and the room and its voice chat carry on. The room server (the VPS service in `deploy/`) handles rooms, their members, who holds the streaming role, signaling and heartbeats; playback commands, video and voice do not pass through it. Established playback continues if the room server becomes unavailable, but nobody new can enter until it returns. These rooms are room protocol 2. The room server also still serves protocol 1, the hosted rooms of older apps, in the same rooms: a room works for one kind of app at a time, and someone on the other kind is told to update, or that the others need to. A newer app says when the server itself needs updating. A dropped connection to the streamer pauses the viewer and is rebuilt automatically.

The app includes a default HTTPS room-service address. Its availability and public-network playback must be validated separately before release. An HTTP localhost address supports testing on this computer. See [server deployment](deploy/README.md).

The Windows **Home** tab opens with **Continue watching**, suggestions for unstarted movies, shows, and anime, and a **Mystery pick** from available titles. Continue watching resumes paused movies or opens the next unwatched episode of a started show. **Refresh picks** reshuffles suggestions and the mystery card. Someone browsing the streamer's library sees the same Home sections for that library and its saved progress; their playback starts in the room.

Local playback remembers your position and volume. Details offer **Resume** and **Start over**; **Return to player** in the sidebar keeps playback reachable while browsing. The player's queue menu includes an editable, persistent queue with episode labels, reordering, removal, and automatic advancement; with no queue loaded, it lists the playing show's episodes so you can jump to another. A button that copies the room's code appears in the top bar when connected to a room, and the bottom bar's **Participants** pill shows who is in the room.

In a room, choose **Voice** in the player's top bar or **Join** in the **Participants** menu to talk while you watch. Voice is optional per person: only people who join voice hear each other, the menu lists who is in voice, and an avatar ring lights up while someone talks. **V** mutes and unmutes your microphone (or, with push-to-talk on, sends your voice while held); the speaker icon on a participant's row mutes them just for you, and the slider beside it sets their level for you (up to 200%, remembered by name). The voice volume slider in the Participants menu applies as it moves. The movie fades to half volume while someone speaks. Settings › **Voice chat** chooses the microphone and output (Windows default follows device changes), shows a live microphone level, and controls microphone processing (Windows voice processing with echo cancellation, noise suppression and gain control, on by default so speakers playing the movie are not sent back to the room; Standard; or Raw), push-to-talk, sending the microphone continuously, auto-join, voice volume and lowering the video. Speech is detected in the voice band against the measured background with 100 ms of pre-roll and 600 ms of hold; listeners prebuffer 60 to 200 ms depending on measured arrival jitter. Joining tells the room at once while the devices open, and the panel shows which step a join is waiting on. Voice uses Opus at about 32 kbit/s over an unreliable, unordered WebRTC channel; each speaker sends straight to everyone else in voice, so voice does not depend on who is playing a video, and each listener enforces mute and a real-time rate limit. Run `dotnet run --project tests/BlackPearl.Smoke -c Release -- --voice` for the codec, jitter buffer and live multi-guest room checks, and `dotnet run --project tests/BlackPearl.DesktopChecks -c Release -- --voice-ui` / `-- --voice-devices` for the window and real audio device checks (device checks play at zero session volume).

BlackPearl also opens videos like a regular player: the installer adds it to **Open with** and Windows **Default apps** for the video types it scans, and videos can be dropped on the window. A video the library knows (the same file, a copy with the same name and size, or another release of the same episode or movie) plays as that library title, with its progress, details page and next episode. Other videos play on their own and remember their position. Opening a video while BlackPearl runs plays it in the open window.

Headset and keyboard media buttons can play or pause the current video, including while BlackPearl is minimized or another app has focus. Windows media controls show the current title and playback state. Room playback buttons follow the streamer's shared-controls setting.

**Search** in the sidebar (Ctrl+F) searches the whole library by title, original title, episode title or file name, and narrows it by type (movies, shows, anime or music), audio language, subtitle language (or no subtitles), genre, decade, length, rating and watch state. A show matches a file filter when any of its episodes does, and its card says how many do. Audio and subtitle languages come from the Matroska and MP4 track headers, from languages identified earlier for untagged tracks, and from the names of subtitle files beside a video; BlackPearl reads them in the background the first time Search opens, caches them per file, and shows matches as files are read. Other containers, such as AVI, only count their subtitle files. Someone searching another member's library can search by name and type. The library's categories list everything in them, without a search field. Missing files have an **Unavailable** filter and disabled playback actions. Every show and film starts under **Unmatched** and **All**, and moves to Shows, Anime or Movies once a metadata lookup or **Find artwork / fix match** identifies it. A rescan recognizes a file moved or renamed within the library folders and keeps its watch progress, queue entries and metadata. **Correct title** works without a metadata token and survives rescanning. Folder categories can be edited in place; folder changes trigger scanning, and an active scan can be cancelled while keeping videos already indexed.

A folder with the **Music** category lists audio files (MP3, FLAC, M4A, AAC, Ogg, Opus, WAV, WMA, AIFF, APE, WavPack, MKA, Musepack) under **Music** in the sidebar, apart from the video library, and video folders keep listing only videos. To list audio files in **Mixed** folders as well, turn on **Mixed folders also list music** in **Folders**; it is off by default. Tracks are grouped into albums by folder, named from their tags, or from `Artist\Album\01 - Title` names when untagged, and use the album's `cover.jpg`/`folder.jpg` or embedded artwork. Disc folders such as `CD 2` join their album. Music never looks up film or show metadata and stays out of **All** and Home. While a song plays, the player shows its artwork, title, artist and album, keeps the controls on screen, lists the album's tracks, and plays the next track straight away. Songs always start from the beginning. Everyone watching a song someone plays for the room sees the same view.

Acquisition plugins can get missing and upcoming episodes, seasons and films. **Acquire** hands a plugin that supports the title's type (anime, show or movie) the video's name, the folder where it belongs and the library folders with their categories. **Settings > Plugins** installs, updates and removes published plugins from [BlackPearlPlugins](https://github.com/Svovoniks/BlackPearlPlugins). See [acquisition plugins](PLUGINS.md).

**Auto-download new episodes** on a show's page follows it: BlackPearl finds each new episode once it airs, using the show's interactive acquisition plugin, and asks before downloading. Following a show opens the Acquire picker once to choose and check where its releases come from, and offers to get the episodes of the latest season that are already out. **Settings > Downloads** turns the hourly check on or off, chooses between **Ask me first** (the default) and **Download after a countdown** of 2, 5 or 15 minutes, and lists the episodes waiting for you, the followed shows and recent downloads. The check also runs while BlackPearl is closed, from a Task Scheduler task for your Windows account, and Windows notifications offer **Download**, **Review** and **Skip**. Only the releases shown in the notification are downloaded. With **Put an icon on the desktop when new episodes are ready** on, an icon such as *2 new episodes - BlackPearl* also appears while episodes wait for you; opening it downloads the episodes it lists, and it goes away once they're downloaded or skipped. Works on Windows 10 (1809 or later) and Windows 11.

## Build and test

Requires the .NET 10 SDK on Windows x64. This workspace has a local SDK in `.tools/dotnet`.

Publishing downloads and verifies Microsoft's signed Visual C++ runtime and restores a pinned WiX extraction tool into `.tools`. The payload hash is pinned in `scripts/copy-native-runtime.ps1`; if Microsoft's download changes, verify and update the hash deliberately. The extracted runtime is included in both portable and installer builds. `native-runtime.json` records its version and source.

```powershell
./build.ps1
./scripts/test.ps1
./build.ps1 -Publish
./scripts/package.ps1 -SkipPublish
```

Run the local coordination service with `./scripts/run-server.ps1`. Open two app instances using separate `BLACKPEARL_DATA` directories to test two members of a room independently. Tests generate their own media and never scan personal folders.

The smoke executable validates SQLite scans, byte ranges and authorization, real native WebRTC transfers, remote LibVLC AVI playback and seeking, entering rooms, the streaming role changing hands, streamer-only playback, automatic buffering recovery, and removal from a room. It starts a temporary loopback server and shuts it down afterward.

Desktop checks exercise media keyboard/app commands and room permissions with generated video. They run on a separate hidden Windows desktop so their windows never appear on yours; set `BLACKPEARL_TEST_VISIBLE=1` to watch them. On an interactive Windows desktop, run `dotnet run --project tests/BlackPearl.DesktopChecks -c Release -- --system-media-controls` to also verify play, pause, and toggle through the Windows media service while the test app is minimized.

## Playback diagnostics

Set `BLACKPEARL_DIAGNOSTICS` to an isolated directory before starting the app to write `diagnostics.jsonl`. Logging is off by default and disk writes run off the UI/native callback threads. Events include playback revisions, raw and briefly interpolated positions, rate-setting results, seeks and settling completion, buffering, range-read latency, clock correction, and dropped-log counts. Separate directories are required for separate app instances.

The default synchronization policy retains the 1,200 ms seek threshold and ±3% rate correction. Set `BLACKPEARL_SYNC_EXPERIMENTAL=1` to test the tighter policy: interpolate between VLC time events for at most 500 ms, use bounded proportional rate correction (0.95–1.05), and seek above 600 ms of per-client error. The tighter policy met the measured H.264/AAC public-room target but caused repeated corrections on the original silent AVI; it is therefore opt-in. Seek settling waits for native timeline advancement with a two-second fallback. Room replay resets an ended native input before starting it, then waits for native readiness before applying the revision. The measured fixture target is a pair-position p95 of at most 500 ms; this is not a guarantee for arbitrary peers, codecs, displayed frames or audio.

Room AVI playback selects VLC's `avformat` demuxer because its native AVI demuxer exposes coarse read-ahead time over HTTP.

## Current implementation boundaries

- Original-file streaming and remote seeking work in automated same-machine tests. A Windows host/VM public-room H.264/AAC run met the experimental 500 ms p95 pair-position target. Silent AVI still fails that target; forced-TURN and wider multi-machine/media coverage remain unvalidated.
- External `.srt`, `.ass`, and `.ssa` sidecars matching the video filename are shared with the room. Embedded subtitles, audio tracks and fonts are delivered as part of the original file. Full HEVC/AV1/ASS/PGS compatibility still needs a representative media corpus.
- Videos are always streamed as the original file; BlackPearl does not transcode or prepare smaller copies. Episode thumbnails are decoded with the bundled LibVLC when no episode image is available.
- Letting others browse your library defaults off for each saved room. Library browsing reveals metadata/thumbnails; video bytes remain authorized only for the selected video and subtitles, and only to people in the room. Anyone with a room's invitation can enter it, so share invitations as you would a private link; the room's creator can remove a member, who then cannot re-enter until let back in.
- The UI has dark/light themes. The shell uses software rendering for reliable WPF/native-video composition; libVLC retains hardware decoding.
- Windows update checks and a per-user GitHub installer are available; see [builds and updates](UPDATES.md). Publisher code-signing credentials are not configured.

## Data and privacy

Library/settings: `%LOCALAPPDATA%/BlackPearl`, or `BLACKPEARL_DATA` when set. On Windows, the TMDB key is stored in Windows Credential Manager when entered in Settings; clearing the field removes it. Poster searches send titles to TVmaze/Wikipedia, or TMDB when configured; movie files stay on the computer that plays them. Disable automatic artwork in Settings to stop these lookups. While auto-download is on, a Task Scheduler task named **BlackPearl episode check** runs `BlackPearl.exe --check-episodes` every hour for your account. Each run reads the followed shows' episode lists from TMDB or TVmaze and asks their acquisition plugin for releases. Turning auto-download off or uninstalling removes the task. The loopback media bridge uses an unguessable per-session path and serves authorized media IDs. Room signaling uses HTTPS/WebSockets; media uses WebRTC encryption, with TURN relay fallback when configured.

Saved rooms keep their invitation until every member forgets them; a room holds up to eight people at once. Relay bandwidth and the streamer's upload increase with each viewer. A public server and a tested relay are necessary for dependable internet use.
